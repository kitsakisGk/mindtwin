\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{E1: End-to-end operation (RQ1)}
\label{sec:results-e1}

We streamed every engine of the four C-MAPSS test sets (407 engines, 104{,}897 cycles) into a
fleet of twins, with engines joining at random start times within the first 100 ticks. Two
configurations were measured: the twin core alone (validation, state and history) and the full
stack, which additionally registers every engine in the fleet knowledge graph and computes
per-condition-normalised trend features (10-cycle mean, standard deviation and slope of every
informative sensor) on every cycle, as the cognition layer will. Table~\ref{tab:e1} reports
throughput and per-cycle latency on a single CPU core.

The twin core processes 29{,}000--48{,}000 cycles per second with a median latency of
\SI{6}{\micro\second}, and the full stack about 300 cycles per second with a median latency of
\SI{3.2}{\milli\second} and a 99th percentile below \SI{10}{\milli\second}. Latency does not grow
with fleet size or the number of operating conditions: FD004 (248 engines, six conditions) is as
fast per cycle as FD001 (100 engines, one condition). The knowledge graph adds 160 triples per
engine (39{,}680 for FD004). Since a real engine produces one cycle per flight, i.e.\ hours apart,
even the full stack on one core has several orders of magnitude of headroom for real-time
operation of large fleets. Live feature computation accounts for over 99\% of the full-stack time;
profiling during development showed that per-condition normalisation implemented with pandas
alone cost \SI{10}{\milli\second} per call, and replacing it with array operations (with
identical outputs) made the full stack 4.3 times faster.
